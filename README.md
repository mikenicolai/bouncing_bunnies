# Bouncing Bunnies

A small, original side-scrolling platform game made with the HTML5 Canvas API. It has no external runtime dependencies or borrowed game assets.

Play in an external browser: <https://mikenicolai.github.io/bouncing_bunnies/>.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder locally:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Then visit <http://localhost:8080>.

- Move: left/right arrow keys (or `A` / `D`)
- Jump: `W`, up arrow, or space. Tap again quickly (up to twice) for a higher jump.
- Hit: `X` or `F`
- Duck: hold down arrow / `S`, or hold the **DUCK** touch button. Release to stand. Ducking preserves foot position, reduces the hurt box from 58 to 18 pixels, limits movement, and blocks jumping/attacking.
- Restart: `R`
- Drag the on-screen joystick with a finger or mouse; use the on-screen jump button to hop.

Text selection, image dragging and long-press callouts are disabled on the game interface, including its menus and controls.

Phones reserve an opaque gray dock below the playing field for Jump, Hit, Duck, the joystick and equipped-weapon controls. Portrait land sits near 86% of the playing field height, with continuous painted soil down to the dock. Cameras use the field height so underwater movement, climbs and the volcano drop stay above the controls. The compact landscape dock also keeps touch controls clear of the world; rotation clears held inputs and reframes the scene. Fullscreen and Home Screen mode respect the available viewport and iPhone safe areas.

## Art direction

Finishing a level opens the world map. **Air → Play Air level** launches Cloud Paths; **Fire → Play Fire level** launches Ember Pass; **Water → Play Water level** launches Tide Pools; **Plants / Earth → Play Earth level** launches Wildwood; **First Meadow → Playable now** returns to the original meadow. The World map button pauses the current run, which can be continued. The final villain map remains a future destination. The Air monster is separate from that final villain.

## Plants / Earth — Wildwood

Wildwood is a 6,900-pixel-wide rising route with 25 branch and mushroom landings, matching Cloud Paths in horizontal scale. A continuous painted grass floor rises through seven sections. Cliffs stop the bunny from simply running through, so the route climbs the broad mossy branches grown into large oaks and beeches. The trees' trunks reach the ground. Yellow and blue mushrooms bounce the bunny at 730 pixels/second, lower than Air's 850-pixel/second trampolines. Spiked red mushrooms are instant-death hazards; reaching the gate at the top completes Earth. Six tree goblins guard the route. They run toward a nearby bunny at the bunny's 260-pixel/second ground speed and can be punched or stomped twice. Far away, each two-to-four-second animation choice is dance 70% of the time and idle 30% of the time. Runtime art: `assets/wildwood-trees-v2.png`, `assets/wildwood-ground-v1.png`, `assets/wildwood-mushrooms-v1.png`, and `assets/tree-goblin-v1.png`; the goblin's [idle, walk, and dance preview](previews/tree-goblin-animation.html) remains available. Run `node tests/earth.cjs` for route and interaction checks.

## Air — Cloud Paths

The Air playfield has no written tutorial signs; clouds, storms, monsters and the wind gate are discovered through play. Press **M** at any time to open the world map and switch between playable levels. The Jump, Hit and Duck touch buttons have translucent fills so the bunny stays visible behind them.

Cloud Paths is a 6,900-pixel-wide route with 23 active platforms that climbs 2,130 pixels (roughly four landscape screen heights) to the summit arena. After the introductory clouds, successive white clouds, blue clouds and trampoline islands lead upward. The camera follows vertically once the bunny reaches the upper or lower part of the view, with smooth movement and fixed HUD/touch controls. Falls below a high checkpoint return both bunny and camera to that safe cloud. White clouds are solid and set safe recovery checkpoints. Four selected solid platforms use the approved flat painted islands: daisies, red/pink flowers, lavender/yellow flowers, and a background tree. The three upper islands are 50 pixels longer (370, 370 and 270 pixels); the starting island stays 280 pixels wide. Their flat tops align with solid landing geometry, with decorative foliage and rocks outside collision. Other white clouds, blue sinking clouds and the high-jump strength is preserved. Source artwork and integration details are in `assets/floating-islands-v2.png` and `assets/floating-islands-v2.md`. Preview all four with the actual game renderer at `previews/islands-v2.html`. White clouds use the approved flat painted silhouette. Blue clouds use matching wavy cyan artwork with a translucent foreground layer drawn after the bunny, so the bunny remains visible through the mist while passing through. Source: `assets/painted-clouds-v1.png`; preview: `previews/painted-clouds.html`. Jump upward through them; descending briefly settles for 0.14 seconds, then sinks at 45 pixels/second through a 60-pixel cloud before normal falling resumes. Press Jump during contact or sinking to escape; movement and crouching do not stop sinking. The early recovery cloud below the first blue cloud has been removed; jump from that blue cloud directly to the first trampoline. Sinking through now drops into open sky, as on later blue clouds. Its retired record retains ID 2 for stable route references and is excluded from rendering, collision and cloud ownership. The first white cloud after the first trampoline is 40 pixels higher, so the spring is required; the two starting-cloud coins are 45 pixels higher and visible above the cloud. Other platform geometry is unchanged. Four former green spring clouds are now wider trampoline islands, each extended by 50 pixels on both sides while retaining its old centre and height. The middle 46% of each island launches the bunny at 850 pixels/second when its foot centre lands or walks onto the pad; the grassy sides are firm ground. The pad compresses, rebounds and settles over 0.4 seconds, with rocks and flowers held still. Source animation sheet and details: `assets/trampoline-island-v1.png` and `assets/trampoline-island-v1.md`; live preview: `previews/trampoline-island.html`. The original two extra jump taps remain available.

A hidden 180-pixel painted island sits directly below the wind gate, 180 pixels beneath its cloud. Jump past the portal and steer back after dropping off the right edge to find it and its two coins. The camera follows down to reveal it. Two quick extra jump taps let the bunny return upward through the gate cloud to the portal; visiting the island does not finish Air or replace the summit checkpoint. Ground to the right of the portal is safe until the bunny actually touches the gate. Three lightning hazards use the approved eight-frame transparent storm-cloud sheet (`assets/storm-cloud-v1-source.png`, provenance in `assets/storm-cloud-v1.md`), embedded in the HTML. Cloud bodies always hurt on contact, including while quiet. The faster three-second cycle has 1.7 seconds quiet, 0.55 seconds charging, a 120ms strike and 100ms fade, then quiet until the cycle repeats. The cloud's internal animation is the only visual warning: no ground outline or charging label. Only full-strike frames damage the downward 34×130px lane. Frame-driven glow, charge/strike audio and particles remain synchronized. Each hit costs one life and grants 1.7 seconds of immunity. Tempest uses the supplied eight-frame transparent sprite sheet (`assets/tempest-v1.png`) and now has ten hearts. Find eight glowing wind ninja stars on the painted ledge beneath the summit cloud: turn back off the summit's left lip, drop onto the nook, and explore beneath the overhang. The stars are visible without a sign or marker. Return left to the blue approach cloud, then use two extra jump taps to get back up. X/F throws a star horizontally; Z equips or stows the stars, and both modes allow jumping. On touch screens use THROW and STOW/EQUIP. Stowing restores punches for the summoned monsters. The eight inventory icons dim while a star is flying, recoverable or lodged. Three spare stars allow extra throws; Tempest still needs five successful lodged hits. A missed or deflected star settles on the nearest solid cloud and is recovered by touch; hitting a monster also leaves the star recoverable. Stars lodged in Tempest never return.

Each successful star hit breaks up to three hearts. His second hit stops at five hearts, triggers a visible 0.9-second recharge, then heals once to seven. The remaining three hits defeat him: 7 → 4 → 1 → 0. His core cannot be defeated with punches, although punching interrupts him for 1.5 seconds. Stars hitting a stun or recharge shield are deflected and remain recoverable. In the stronger phase he patrols faster, fires paired gusts every 1.45 seconds instead of a single gust every 2.4 seconds, and summons existing three-heart cloud monsters after a visible wind swirl, with at most two alive at once. Wind curls on his hand warn of each shot. Duck under either pattern or jump over it; boosted jumps clear the paired gusts. Five embedded stars remain visible on the boss. Defeating him clears his gusts and summoned monsters and opens the summit's downdraft passage. His body/wind column still hurts, and his tail rises after 30 seconds to allow crawling underneath, but the exit stays closed until he is defeated.

The descent retains four alternating cloud barriers and three smaller lightning clouds, with controlled falling speed, the blue waypoint, and the wind gate. Missed landings recover at the summit. Ninja-star ownership, lodged hits and boss phase persist after a fall; R starts a fresh run. Painted star artwork and its exact built-in imagegen prompt are in `assets/wind-ninja-star-v1.png` and `assets/wind-ninja-star-v1.md`. Practice in the actual game at `previews/tempest-wind-stars.html`. ## Water · Tide Pools

The new Water route begins with a short land jump and then opens into a deep pool. The bunny changes to a painted eight-pose swimming sheet based on the original character and the user's dive sketch. Its swim cycle alternates paws, kicks and ear bends; diving and underwater hitting use separate poses. Source and pose mapping: `assets/pink-ear-bunny-swim-v1.png` and `assets/pink-ear-bunny-swim-v1.md`. Underwater, Arrow keys/WASD or the touch joystick move in all four directions; Jump/Space gives an upward swim stroke, and Down or the Dive touch button moves downward. At the surface, Jump/Space launches the bunny into the air; it falls back into the water with a splash, and Dive brings it down sooner. Three reefs hang from the surface. The bunny can dive beneath them and surface in the clear pools between them. Colorful branching coral, waving seagrass, fish schools, and slow turtles bring the sea floor to life without adding hazards.

The air meter lasts 15 seconds underwater and refills immediately when the bunny reaches the surface. Running out of air costs one heart and returns the bunny to the most recent safe surface pool. Four patrolling sharks have two hearts each. Their eight-pose painted sheet follows the user's pencil shark: a four-phase tail sweep and a mouth-opening bite cycle. Source and pose mapping: `assets/shark-swim-v1.png` and `assets/shark-swim-v1.md`. Three pirate skeletons guard the sea floor. Their painted eight-pose sheet follows the user's checked-bandana, orange-clothed pirate drawing: small planted steps and a hook-and-cutlass swing that can hit the bunny on either side. Each pirate remains within 30 pixels of its anchor; Hit interrupts the swing and stuns it. Source and pose mapping: `assets/pirate-skeleton-v1.png` and `assets/pirate-skeleton-v1.md`. Reach the glowing underwater gate to finish. The bunny, shark and pirate sheets are embedded; the rest of the water scene is drawn by Canvas, so the runtime remains self-contained. Run `node tests/water.cjs` to check animation selection, water controls, oxygen, enemies, and route reachability.

The wreck is now a physical obstacle. Two painted sealed air cabins have solid roof beams and 32-unit side posts. Four clearly visible floor hatches connect the lower hold to the rooms; each room has a separate entry and exit hatch. The continuous deck blocks every other upward route, and the solid keel blocks entry from below. Broken bow and stern doorways open into the lower hold. The open water above the wreck remains a bypass, giving neither cabin air nor the ship key. Head-above-water breathing and key collection work inside the enclosed cabins; the local water surface is drawn between their sealed side posts. Decorative fish avoid the new timber and cabin spaces. The painted cabin PNG is `assets/pirate-air-cabin-v1.png`, with its exact built-in imagegen prompt in `assets/pirate-air-cabin-v1.md`. `previews/ship-cabin-review.html` shows the whole wreck and a toggleable coral/mint overlay for blocked timber and open passages. ## Fire — Ember Pass

Fire now spans 6,900 world units, matching Air and Earth. The outdoor opening has five modest parkour gaps, painted ember trees, and four slow zombies. The trees use the Wildwood brushwork, with black wood and fiery red leaves. Their roots are buried in the surface and volcano slope; trunks, branches and crowns are harmless decoration. Painted crimson soil, volcanic roots and warm embers from `assets/ember-ground-v2.png` cover the opening platforms and fill the volcano. A buried antique bunny bust, paw casting and urn hide in the earth. Zombies walk through coins without collecting them; their patrols extend to the platform edges. The bunny runs on all four paws up a 1,500-unit volcano slope and can jump in either direction. Three painted stone variants roll downhill; higher slope-aligned jumps clear them, while contact costs one life with normal immunity. Spawns leave room to react and stop before the summit. The camera keeps the bunny about one-third from the left while running and jumping, and follows the terrain vertically so the jump remains visible. Airborne poses retain the slope angle and change phase relative to the rising ground. At the summit it drops through a 2,950-unit crater, steering between four alternating lava-topped rock barriers at a controlled fall speed. A failed drop returns to the summit checkpoint. A short walk across the crater floor leads to the entrance of the final 25×15-cell labyrinth; there is no opening maze or outdoor section after it. Finding the bow and reaching the maze exit opens the second volcano and the Lava King encounter.

The labyrinth uses an 80-pixel grid, character-sized corridors, ladder rungs, and interconnected loops around zombies. Arrow keys/WASD and the touch joystick walk or climb in four directions; jumping is available on the volcano through keyboard, touch button and upward joystick pulls. Ducking is disabled on the slope; both jumping and ducking are disabled in the crater and maze. Four slow maze zombies patrol; only the two nearest within seven cells pursue. All eight zombies share the supplied drawing’s painted shaggy hair, forward blank white eye, torn sleeves, dark clothes, and red shorts. Punches reduce their displayed hearts and briefly stun them. Source: `assets/lava-zombie-v3.png` and `assets/lava-zombie-v3.md`. `previews/fire-cavern-review.html` shows each stage in the actual renderer, and `previews/lava-zombie-animation.html` shows the walk cycle. `node tests/fire.cjs` checks the opening and crater routes at 30/60/120 FPS on phone and desktop widths, jumping ascent, summit recovery, harmless trees, zombie passage through coins, zombie combat, alternate maze paths, and completion.


## Verification

### iPhone and joystick controls

Portrait phones now use a 480-unit-wide camera and a tall responsive canvas, with a separate bottom control panel. Landscape returns to the 960×540 view without restarting the level. Route tests cover both widths at 30/60/120 FPS. To remove Safari's URL bar, use **Share → Add to Home Screen → Open as Web App**, then launch the saved icon; a webpage cannot force Safari's own controls away. The game remains one self-contained HTML runtime.

Fullscreen uses the native API where available, otherwise a fixed edge-to-edge CSS view sized to the visual viewport, with safe-area padding, orientation/resize updates and scroll restoration on exit. iPhone Safari can retain its browser bars: the title-screen hint recommends **Share → Add to Home Screen** for the best fullscreen experience. Saved standalone apps start fitted to the available viewport. The exit toggle always remains available; this does not promise to hide Safari chrome.

The lower-right stick moves horizontally and diagonally. Pull up beyond 60% for one Jump; return above the −25% neutral boundary before pulling up again. Existing two-boost limits still apply. ## Five-arrow bow upgrade

A fresh Fire run chooses a reachable labyrinth corridor different from the previous pickup location. Finding the bow unlocks five arrow slots at the top of the screen. Each shot spends one slot; one spent slot refills every three seconds of active play, including with the bow stowed. Pausing freezes the timer, checkpoints preserve ammunition and pickup location, and a fresh run resets both. Every initial and returned arrow independently rolls normal (89%, one heart), fire (10%, two hearts), or rainbow (1%, three hearts). The HUD previews the stored types and marks the next shot. Special damage applies to zombies and the Lava King, respecting existing stun and shield rules.

Original transparent artwork and the built-in ImageGen prompt: [assets/enchanted-arrow-types-v1.md](assets/enchanted-arrow-types-v1.md). See the image, inventory and recharge animation in [previews/arrow-upgrade-review.html](previews/arrow-upgrade-review.html). Run `node tests/arrow-upgrade.cjs` for pickup reachability, exact probability boundaries, ammunition limits, three-second returns and damage at 30/60/120 FPS.

# Bouncing Bunnies

A small, original side-scrolling platform game made with the HTML5 Canvas API. It has no external runtime dependencies or borrowed game assets.

Play in an external browser: <https://mikenicolai.github.io/bouncing_bunnies/>.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder locally:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Then visit <http://localhost:8080>.

- Move: left/right arrow keys (or `A` / `D`)
- Jump: `↑` or `Space` on the keyboard. Tap either jump key again quickly (up to twice) for a higher jump, including in the Castle. The two extra taps must follow a ground jump within 0.52 seconds. Landing restores the sequence; Castle monster bounces do not refill it. With the bow equipped, Up aims and Space jumps, including the two limited boosts.
- Hit: `X` or `F`
- Duck: hold down arrow / `S`, or hold the **DUCK** touch button. Release to stand. Ducking preserves foot position, reduces the hurt box from 58 to 18 pixels, limits movement, and blocks jumping/attacking.
- Drag the on-screen joystick with a finger or mouse; use the on-screen jump button to hop.

Text selection, image dragging and long-press callouts are disabled on the game interface, including its menus and controls.

Phones reserve an opaque gray dock below the playing field for Jump, Hit, Duck, the joystick and equipped-weapon controls. Portrait land sits near 86% of the playing field height, with continuous painted soil down to the dock. Cameras use the field height so underwater movement, climbs and the volcano drop stay above the controls. The compact landscape dock also keeps touch controls clear of the world; rotation clears held inputs and reframes the scene. Fullscreen and Home Screen mode respect the available viewport and iPhone safe areas.

## Coins and continuing after death

Every collected gold bunny coin adds one coin to a shared wallet, shown beneath the run score and on the world map. The wallet persists across worlds, restarts and browser reloads through local storage; blocked storage still allows earning and spending during the current session. The HUD labels the spendable balance as **available** and the level count as **collected**, with the equation **starting wallet + collected − spent = available**. Buying a heart retains the collected count and records 20 coins spent. A full level restart resets the collection count and starts a new equation with the current wallet balance. A test ad spends no coins.

At zero hearts, the free option starts a new journey in Meadow and resets world progression; the shared coin wallet is kept. Spending 20 coins continues from the last checkpoint with one heart, retaining collected coins, puzzles, equipment, ammunition and boss progress. The five-second local test ad restarts the current level from its beginning with three hearts and resets its coins, puzzles, enemies and bosses. Both options keep earlier completed worlds. A reload after game over also starts in Meadow; a successful paid continuation/ad restart saves the retained earlier-world progress again. Developer practice leaves normal saves untouched. The ad freezes gameplay and input until all five seconds have elapsed, and stale callbacks cannot grant another restart.

This is a local ad simulation with no real advertisement or CrazyGames SDK. Insufficient funds disable the coin purchase. [Practice the Fire game-over choices without changing your normal saves](previews/coin-revive-review.html). `tests/coin-revive.cjs` covers all collection/death paths, persistence, checkpoint continuation, ad level restarts and fallback at 30/60/120 FPS. `tests/coin-revive-browser.cjs` checks actual buttons, the real five-second wait, phone layouts, and reload behavior.

## Art direction

Normal play follows **First Meadow → Air → Water → Plants / Earth → Fire → Castle**. Clear the earlier worlds before entering a later one. Finishing a level opens the map, which shows earned checks and offers the next destination; clicking its painted destinations does not select a different level. Opening the map during play pauses the current run and provides a Continue button. Reloading recommends the first unfinished world, preserving earned progress and the coin wallet. The Air monster is separate from the final villain.

While on the map, press **D** to open **Developer mode**. This shortcut is intentionally absent from the normal controls and map. The plain text screen shows version, build number, build date and buttons for the five worlds plus the Castle opening. Set a test wallet from **0 to 1,000 coins**, or use the **100 / 1,000** quick buttons. Developer runs show a small mode label; their coins and completed levels are not saved to the normal adventure. **Return to normal map** or Escape restores the original wallet and leaves testing. Opening/closing the screen without launching a test preserves the paused normal run; returning after launching a test resumes normal progression at its next world. Reloading also exits testing. Set `DEVELOPER_TOOLS_ENABLED=false` in `assets/developer-mode-v1.js` and synchronize the embedded module to remove developer entry points for production. `tests/developer-mode.cjs` and `tests/developer-mode-browser.cjs` cover ordered progression, all test destinations, save isolation, wallet bounds, controls, phone layouts and the disabled flag.

## First Meadow — shooting and diving lessons

Meadow now runs for 7,500 units. Its original jumping route leads into a practice shooting range: collect the bow, use **Z / EQUIP** to draw or stow it, **↑ / ↓** to aim, and **X / F / FIRE** to shoot. Three tethered balloons must each be hit by an arrow before the wooden door rises. Misses leave the targets intact; five ordinary arrows recharge one at a time every three seconds. Popping the last balloon stows the bow so the bunny can continue walking. The practice bow can also shoot Meadow monsters: one arrow defeats a one-heart monster, with the same death animation as a punch or stomp. Walls and the wooden door block arrows, and each shot stops at its first hit. `node tests/meadow-archery.cjs` checks these interactions at 30/60/120 FPS.

Leave the practice bow at the rack before the pond. **↓ / S / DIVE** or a downward joystick pull swims beneath the rock passage; surface swimming and jumps cannot bypass it. **↑ / W** swims up and **Space / STROKE** gives an upward stroke. The pond has 15 seconds of air, a safe shore checkpoint and a sloping far bank that returns the bunny to walking. The pond banks use continuous, overlapping texture patches with an uneven sand-to-soil blend; soil continues below the original image depth, including tall phone views. The relocated shrine completes Meadow only after the balloon and diving lessons. Restart resets both lessons; drowning keeps the opened door and returns the bunny to the shore.

[Practice each section](previews/meadow-lessons.html), [review the shooting range](previews/meadow-shooting-range-review.png), or [see the dive passage](previews/meadow-diving-review.png). `node tests/meadow-tutorial.cjs` checks real arrow shots, gate blocking, the required unarmed dive, exit and completion at 30/60/120 FPS on phone/desktop. ## Verification

### iPhone and joystick controls

Portrait phones now use a 480-unit-wide camera and a tall responsive canvas, with a separate bottom control panel. Landscape returns to the 960×540 view without restarting the level. Route tests cover both widths at 30/60/120 FPS. To remove Safari's URL bar, use **Share → Add to Home Screen → Open as Web App**, then launch the saved icon; a webpage cannot force Safari's own controls away. The game remains one self-contained HTML runtime.

Fullscreen uses the native API where available, otherwise a fixed edge-to-edge CSS view sized to the visual viewport, with safe-area padding, orientation/resize updates and scroll restoration on exit. iPhone Safari can retain its browser bars: the title-screen hint recommends **Share → Add to Home Screen** for the best fullscreen experience. Saved standalone apps start fitted to the available viewport. The exit toggle always remains available; this does not promise to hide Safari chrome.
